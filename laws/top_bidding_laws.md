# Top Bidding Laws in Duplicate Bridge

Based on the *Laws of Duplicate Bridge 2017*, the following are the most critical and frequently cited laws governing the auction (bidding) period.

## 1. Law 16: Authorized and Unauthorized Information
One of the most complex and frequently invoked laws, especially regarding hesitations.
*   **Authorized Information**: Information a player is entitled to use, such as the auction history, the cards played, and their own hand.
*   **Unauthorized Information (UI)**: Information gained from outside the legal flow of the game, such as partner's hesitation, tone of voice, or a remark.
*   **The Restriction**: If a player has UI, they must not choose from among "logical alternatives" one that could demonstrably have been suggested by the UI.

## 2. Law 20: Review and Explanation of Calls
This law ensures transparency. Players are entitled to:
*   Have all previous calls restated during their turn to call.
*   Request an explanation of opponents' partnership understandings.
*   **Key Duty**: Partners must explain each other's calls based on their *agreements*, not their own hand.

## 3. Law 21: Misinformation
Protects the non-offending side when they have been misled.
*   If a player makes a call based on an opponent's incorrect explanation, they may be allowed to change their call without penalty (if discovered in time).

## 4. Law 23: Comparable Call
A vital concept for modern bridge rectification.
*   A "comparable call" is one that has a similar meaning or defines a subset of the meanings of a withdrawn call.
*   If an irregular call (like an out-of-turn bid or insufficient bid) is replaced by a comparable call, the auction often continues without further penalty, minimizing the "damage" to the auction flow.

## 5. Law 25: Legal and Illegal Changes of Call
Distinguishes between a "slip of the tongue" and a "change of mind."
*   **Unintended Call (Law 25A)**: If a player makes a mechanical error (e.g., pulling the wrong bid from a box), they can correct it without penalty until their partner calls.
*   **Intended Call (Law 25B)**: If a player changes their mind after making an intentional call, the original call stands and the partner may be forced to pass for the rest of the auction.

## 6. Law 27: Insufficient Bid
One of the most common irregularities.
*   If a player bids at a level lower than required, they have several options:
    1.  Correct it to the lowest sufficient bid in the same denomination (often no penalty).
    2.  Substitute a "comparable call" (often no penalty).
    3.  Make a different sufficient bid or pass (partner may be required to pass for the rest of the auction).

## 7. Laws 29-32: Calls Out of Rotation
Covers passes, bids, and doubles made when it was another player's turn.
*   Rectification depends on whose turn it actually was (RHO, Partner, or LHO) and whether the call was accepted by the next player in rotation.

## 8. Law 40: Partnership Understandings
The fundamental law regarding system disclosure.
*   Partnerships must make their agreements available to opponents (e.g., via Convention Cards).
*   A player may not make a call based on a partnership understanding that has not been disclosed.
*   **Psychic Calls**: Allowed unless they are based on a partnership understanding (see WBF Guidelines for more).
