# Deep Dive: Law 21, Law 75, and ACBL Alert Procedures

This document explores the critical distinction between failures in disclosure and failures in memory, and how the *Laws of Duplicate Bridge 2017* (and specifically ACBL regulations) protect the game.

---

## 1. Law 21: Misinformation
Law 21 provides the general remedy for when a player has been misled by their opponents.

### A. Player's Own Misunderstanding (Law 21A)
If you misunderstand a *legal* call or play (e.g., you forgot the opponents play a particular system, or you just miscounted points), there is **no redress**. You are responsible for your own "bridge errors."

### B. Misinformation from an Opponent (Law 21B)
If your call was influenced by an opponent's failure to provide accurate information:
*   **Trigger**: Failure to alert or a mistaken explanation.
*   **The Remedy (Law 21B1a)**: Until the end of the auction period (and before partner calls), you may **change your call** without penalty. 
*   **Adjusted Score (Law 21B3)**: If it's too late to change the call, the Director awards an **adjusted score** if the offending side gained an advantage.
*   **Presumption (Law 21B1b)**: The Director is to presume **Mistaken Explanation** (the infraction) rather than Mistaken Call (the bridge error).

## 2. Law 75: Mistaken Explanation or Mistaken Call
Law 75 handles the "disconnect" between partners. It defines whether a situation is a "misexplanation" (infraction) or a "misbid" (legal).

### Why is "Misbid" Law 75?
Law 75 exists because bridge is a game of **partnership agreements**. The Law must distinguish between:
1.  **Misexplanation (75B)**: Failure to tell the truth about the agreement.
2.  **Misbid (75C)**: Failure to remember the agreement.

Without Law 75, players could use secret "mistakes" to find the right contract. Law 75 creates the **Unauthorized Information (UI)** trap to keep the game fair.

### A. Mistaken Explanation (Law 75B) - *The Infraction*
*   **Scenario**: Agreement is "2D = GF," but partner explains "2D = Weak."
*   **Status**: **Misinformation**. If opponents are damaged, Director adjusts the score.
*   **Correction**: Explainer corrects immediately; Caller **must not** correct during the auction.

### B. Mistaken Call / Misbid (Law 75C) - *The Bridge Error*
*   **Scenario**: Agreement is "2D = Weak," partner explains "2D = Weak," but the caller actually had a GF hand and forgot.
*   **Status**: **No infraction**. The partnership correctly disclosed their agreement. Memory lapse is a "bridge error."
*   **Outcome**: The result stands.

### C. Unauthorized Information (Law 75A) - *The Trap*
If you hear your partner give a **wrong explanation** of your call (or fail to alert it), you now have UI.
*   You **know** your partner is on a different page.
*   **Obligation**: You **must** bid and play as if you *didn't* hear that explanation. You must continue the auction as if your partner knows exactly what you have. 
*   **Penalty**: If you use the UI to "fix" the auction, the Director awards an **adjusted score**.

## 3. ACBL Alert Procedure (2021 Update)

The ACBL's logic is to **improve disclosure while reducing unnecessary inquiries**. Failing to follow these procedures is legally identical to **Misinformation (Law 21)**.

### A. The Logic: Categories of Calls
1.  **Natural/Expected = Non-Alertable**: No notification needed.
2.  **Highly Specific/Standard = Announceable**: Speak a short phrase immediately.
3.  **Artificial/Unusual = Alertable**: Say "Alert" and tap the strip.

### B. All Announceable Calls (ACBL)
Announcements are mandatory for the following calls. The partner speaks the required phrase immediately.

| Call | Announcement | Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1NT Opening** | State the range (e.g., "15 to 17") | Standard ranges vary; immediate disclosure is most efficient. |
| **Jacoby & Texas Transfers** | "Transfer" | Responses to 1NT, 2NT, or Overcalls that show a major suit. |
| **1NT Response to 1H/1S** | "Forcing" or "Semi-Forcing" | Clarifies if the responder can pass or if the opener must bid. |
| **Inverted Minor Raises** | "Inverted" | Raised minors (e.g., 1C-2C) that are strong and forcing. |
| **Short Club/Diamond** | "Could be [number]" | If the minor opening could be fewer than 3 cards (e.g., "Could be two"). |
| **Transfer Responses to Overcalls** | "Transfer" | If the partnership uses transfers after an opponent's overcall. |

### C. Non-Alertable Calls (No Notification Required)
The logic here is that these calls are **natural**, **universally understood**, or **expected standard practice**. Opponents do not need to be warned about them.

| Call Type | Examples | Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Natural Bids** | 1H opening (5+ Hearts), 2S overcall | Most natural bids at a reasonable level are expected. |
| **Common Response** | 1NT response to 1C/1D | Standard balanced response showing a known range. |
| **Standard Stayman** | 1NT - 2C | So universal it no longer needs an alert. |
| **Strong/Artificial 2C** | 2C opening (22+ pts or GF) | The standard way to show a massive hand; opponents expect this. |
| **Takeout Doubles** | (1H) - Dbl | Standard competitive bid showing the other suits. |
| **Blackwood/Gerber** | 4NT or 4C ace-asking | Standard slam-seeking tools. |
| **Most Cuebids** | (1H) - 2H | Usually shows a big hand with fit; standard bridge logic. |
| **Negative Doubles** | 1C - (1H) - Dbl | Standard way to show unbid majors; universally expected. |

### D. Alertable Calls (Notification Required)
The logic here is that these calls are **highly artificial**, **non-standard**, or have **multiple meanings** that an opponent cannot reasonably guess. Failure to alert these is an infraction under **Law 21**.

| Call Type | Examples | Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Artificial Openings** | Multi 2D, 2-level showing 2 suits | Opponents cannot know which suits/strength are shown. |
| **Support Doubles** | 1C - (Pass) - 1H - (1S) - Dbl | Shows exactly 3-card support; too specific to be "natural." |
| **Unusual NT Defense** | Cappelletti, DONT, Meckwell | Artificial bids over 1NT vary wildly by partnership. |
| **Forcing Passes** | Pass in a GF auction | A pass that requires partner to bid is a special agreement. |
| **Non-Standard Transfers** | 1NT - 2S (as Minor transfer) | Transfers to suits other than H/S (standard) need an alert. |
| **Two-Way Stayman** | 1NT - 2C and 2D as Stayman | If 2D is also a variation of Stayman, it must be alerted. |
| **Fit-Showing Jumps** | 1H - (Pass) - 3S (as fit+strength) | Highly specific meaning combining fit and side-suit length. |
| **Psychic Artificial Bids** | Any psychic use of an artificial bid | Strictly regulated and must be alerted (see WBF guidelines). |

## 4. Summary Table: Law 21 vs Law 75

| Feature | Law 21 (Misinformation) | Law 75 (Mistaken Call/Explanation) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Focus** | Opponents' perspective. | Partnership disconnect. |
| **Trigger** | Incorrect info given to you. | Disconnect between call and explanation. |
| **Outcome** | Right to change call / adjust score. | Defines if it's an infraction or a bridge error. |
| **Key Risk** | Damaging the non-offending side. | Misusing Unauthorized Information (UI). |
