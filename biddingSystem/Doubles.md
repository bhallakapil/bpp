# Doubles

### 1\. Takeout Double

A takeout double is typically made by an **Overcaller** whose opponents have opened the bidding, and the doubler has opening points but no clear suit to bid. It asks partner (the Advancer) to bid their best unbid suit. No Off-shape doubles unless a hand too strong to make a simple overcall(17+)  (10-11 possible with 4441,544, 5431 usually with 2 def tricks)

- **Origin:** The name "takeout" refers to taking out the opponent's bid, asking partner to bid another suit Or a hand too strong to overcall  
- **Purpose:** To show a strong hand (usually 12+ points) and support for all unbid suits, asking partner to choose one.  
- **Context:** Usually made on the first or second round of bidding, after an opponent's suit bid.  
- **Requirements:** Shortness in the doubled suit and support for the other three suits.   
- Any hand with 3 cards of an opponent's suit and up to 14 HCP does not qualify for a direct takeout double.   
- **Example:** Opponent opens 1♠. Your hand: ♠K 2, ♥A K J 10, ♦A Q 9, ♣K Q 8\. You double, showing 12+ points, shortness in spades, and support for the other three suits.

### 2\. Penalty Double (Business Double)

A penalty double is made by any player (often a **Defender**) when they believe the opponents have bid too high and will not make their contract. It increases the score for setting the contract.

- **Origin:** "Penalty" clearly indicates its purpose to penalize opponents. "Business" implies it's a serious, non-informational double.  
- **Purpose:** To penalize opponents for an unmakeable contract.  
- **Context:** Typically made at a high level (e.g., 3NT, 4S) or when the doubler has a strong defensive hand with tricks in the opponent's trump suit.  
- **Requirements:** Strong belief that the contract will fail, often with specific defensive holdings.  
- **Example:** Opponents bid 4♠. Your hand: ♠A K J, ♥Q 10, ♦K J 9 8, ♣A Q 7\. You have strong defensive tricks in spades and double, expecting to set their contract.

### 3\. Negative Double (Sputnik Double)

A negative double is made by the **Responder** after partner opens and an opponent makes an overcall. It shows support for the unbid major suits (if applicable) or a strong hand without a clear natural bid, denying a fit for partner's suit.

- **Origin:** "Negative" refers to the fact that it denies a natural bid in the opponent's suit and often shows a lack of a strong suit of its own, while still showing strength. Also known as "Sputnik Double" after the Soviet satellite, as it was a "new" and "far-reaching" convention.  
- **Purpose:** To show support for unbid major suits (if applicable) or a strong hand, often in competitive auctions, denying a fit for partner's suit.  
- **Context:** Made by the responder after partner opens and an opponent makes an overcall.  
- **Requirements:** enough to force partnership to next level ie Usually 6+ points, often showing 4-card support for an unbid major, or a strong hand without a natural bid.  
- **Example:** 1♣ \- (1♦). Your hand: ♠A Q 10 9, ♥J 8 7 4, ♦5 4, ♣K 7 6\. You double, showing 4-card majors and denying a natural diamond bid.

#### Specific Scenarios for Negative Doubles (Support for Unbid Suits)

Partnerships often have different understandings about the length in unbid suits that is shown by a negative double, and these understandings differ according both to which suits remain unbid and to the current level of the bidding. Nevertheless, the following are popular understandings:

- **After 1♣ – (1♦):** A negative double is often understood to show at least four cards in both hearts (♥) and spades (♠).  
- **After 1m – (1♥):** A popular agreement is that a negative double shows exactly four spades (♠). Responder would bid 1♠ with five or more.  
- **After 1m – (1♠):** In contrast to the prior sequence, most experts prefer the negative double here to show at *least* four hearts (♥) – not *exactly* four hearts.  
- **After 1M – (2m):** Most play that a negative double shows at least four cards in the other major.  
- **After 1♦ – (2♣):** Few play that double shows at least four cards in *each* major, but most play that doubler shows at least one major at least four cards long.  
- **After 1♥ \- (1♠) or 1♠ \- (2♥):** The negative double shows at least 4 cards in each minor because there are no unbid major suits.

### 4\. Responsive Double \- Play over take out double by partner and only

A responsive double is made by the **Advancer** after opponents have found a fit (bid two suits) and asks partner to bid one of the unbid suits. It's a competitive bid, showing general strength and interest in the remaining suits.

- **Origin:** "Responsive" highlights that it's a response to the opponents' bidding, asking partner for a response in turn.  
- **Purpose:** To show general strength (usually 8+ points) and support for the unbid suits, asking partner to choose one.  
- **Context:** Made after opponents have bid two suits, and partner has overcalled (suit or take out dbl).  
- **Requirements:** Usually 8+ points and support for the unbid suits.  
- **Example:** (1♣) \- 1♥ \- (2♣).   
  - Your hand: ♠Q J 10 8, ♥A 8, ♦K Q 7 2, ♣9 5 4\. You double, showing general strength and asking partner to bid spades or diamonds \- We do **not** play above**.**  
  - (1♣) \- x \- (2♣) \- x (responsive) \- We **play** responsive double over take out doubles

### 5\. Support Double/Redouble by Opener

A support double is made by the **Opener** to show 3-card support for responder's major suit after an opponent's overcall.

- **Origin:** "Support" directly indicates its function: to show support for partner's suit.  
- **Purpose:** To show 3-card support for partner's major suit while  a direct raise shows 4 cards  
- **Context:** Made by the opener after responder bids a major suit and an opponent overcalls.  
- **Requirements:** 3-card support for partner's major.  
- **Example:** 1♣ \- P \- 1♥ \- (1♠/X)  Opener's hand: ♠K 8 3, ♥Q 9 7, ♦A J 10, ♣A K Q 2\.   
  - Opener doubles or redouble, showing 3-card heart support.   
  - A direct raise shows 4 card ♥

### 6\. Support Doubles/Redoubles in Competition by Advancer

In competitive auctions, especially when partner has overcalled and both opponents have bid, a double or redouble by **Advancer** can be used to show 3-card support for partner's suit when a direct raise would show 4+ cards. This conflicts with **Responsive** double after suit overcall.

- **Purpose:** To show 3-card support for partner's suit when a direct raise would show 4+ cards.  
- **Context:** Typically occurs after partner makes an overcall, and both opponents have subsequently bid.  
- **Requirements:** 3-card support for partner's suit. A direct raise would typically show 4+ cards.  
- **Example:** (1♦) \- 1♠ \- (2♦/x)  
  - Double/redouble 3 card ♠  
  - 2♠ 4 card suit  
  - For major overcall it is always support x/xx

#### *1C \- 1D Overcall* 

* **X (Majors)**: XX \- Support  
* **1H / 1S**: X \- Negative double (other major)  
* **2C**: X \- Responsive (majors)  
* **3C**: X \- Responsive \- BOP or 5-5+ majors

#### *1D \- 2C Overcall* 

* **X (Majors)**: XX \- Support  
* **2D**: X \- Responsive (majors)  
* **2H**: X \- Negative, must have 3-card support for partner's suit or BOP values (should bid 2S with 3 spades)  
* **2S**: X \- Negative, must have 3+ support for partner's suit AND BOP values (forced to the three level regardless of partner holding)  
* **3D**: X \- Responsive \- BOP or 5-5+ majors

#### *1M \- 2m Overcall* 

* **1H / (2C / 2D)** \-\> **2H**: X \- Negative, must have 3-card support for partner's suit or BOP values (should bid 2S with 3 spades)  
* **1S / (2C / 2D)** \-\> **2S**: X \- Negative, must have 3+ support for partner's suit AND BOP values (forced to the three level regardless of partner holding)

### 7\. Lead-Directing Double

A lead-directing double is made by a **Defender** against a notrump contract to suggest a specific suit for partner to lead.

- **Origin:** The name clearly states its purpose: to direct partner's opening lead.  
- **Purpose:** To ask partner to lead a specific suit against a notrump contract.  
- **Context:** Made by a defender against a notrump contract.  
- **Requirements:** Dummy’s first bid suit or lead unusual.

### 8\. Maximal Overcall Double

A maximal overcall double is a competitive double made by the **Responder** or **Advancer** at the three-level or higher, indicating a desire to play in game, but not necessarily in the doubled suit.

- **Origin:** "Maximal" refers to bidding to the maximum level to reach game, and "overcall" indicates it's in response to an opponent's overcall.  
- **Purpose:** To show a strong hand and a desire to reach game, asking partner to choose a game-going contract.  
- **Context:** Occurs when opponents have bid a suit at a high level, leaving no room for a new suit bid to invite and the doubler's side is competing for game.  
- **Requirements:** Strong hand, usually 10+ points, and a desire for game.

### 9\. Lightner Double

A Lightner double is a penalty double made by a **Defender** against a slam contract, asking partner to lead an unusual suit (often the first unbid suit).

- **Origin:** Named after the American bridge player Theodore Lightner, who popularized this convention.  
- **Purpose:** To ask partner for an unusual lead against a slam contract, typically a suit other than trumps or the opponent's bid suits.  
- **Context:** Made by a defender against an opponent's slam contract.  
- **Requirements:** Strong defensive hand, often with a void.  
- **Example:** Opponents bid 6♠ . Your hand: ♠ x x x, ♥A J 10 x x, ♦Void, ♣Q J 9 8 7\. Double ask partner to find the void and lead it.
